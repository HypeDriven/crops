STARHERMIT CROPS / MODEL DOCUMENTATION

Five crops. Five architectures.

This is a botanically informed procedural study of wheat, two-row barley, maize, sunflower, and soybean. Representative forms are modelled; they are not scans of named cultivars or validated phenotyping instruments.

Seasonal assumptions

Wheat and barley are autumn-sown winter forms: establishment, dormancy, spring extension, then summer grain filling. Maize, sunflower, and soybean are spring-sown annuals: spring establishment and summer reproduction. Their autumn and winter scenes show cut, dead residue after harvest, not living dormancy. Progression is illustrative within each scene, not an agronomic prediction.

Distinct structures

Two-row barley has one fertile central spikelet per triplet, sterile lateral spikelets, smooth clasping auricles, and long lemma awns. Maize has sheathing parallel-veined leaves, a terminal male tassel, axillary husked female ears and silk, and brace roots. Sunflower has opposite lower and alternate upper leaves, serrate reticulate blades, involucral bracts and separate ray and disk florets. Soybean has initial unifoliate leaves, later trifoliate leaves, papilionaceous flowers, hairy pods and a nodulated taproot. Cutaways expose representative maize kernel rows and soybean seeds.

Leaf age and shoot tips

Leaf rank determines relative age: young apical blades are smaller, more folded and less expanded; older basal leaves droop and senesce earlier. Middle leaves may be largest. Vegetative grass tips have emerging rolled blades; young sunflower and soybean shoots retain folded leaves. Mature spikes, tassels and sunflower heads terminate those shoots without new foliage above them. Use the Shoot tip inspection view to examine the apex.

Scale and limitations

Microtexture, surface ridges, veins, and pubescence are procedural visual cues. Stomata, cellular anatomy, pollen grains and internal tissue anatomy are not resolved. Root views truncate the system at 45 cm; maize and sunflower roots naturally extend deeper. Stand density is a display count, not a yield model. Wind deformation is not a calibrated biomechanical simulation. Seed, floret, and nodule counts are representative.
