STARHERMIT CROPS / MODEL DOCUMENTATION

A study of winter wheat.

This is a procedural, botanically informed visual study of an awned winter form of common wheat. It is an educational model, not a scanned cultivar or a validated phenotyping instrument.

A season is a stage, not a colour filter.

Autumn shows establishment and basal tillering; winter retains short, dormant vegetative plants under snow; spring develops jointed culms and leaves; summer develops ears and grain, then senesces. The cycle assumes autumn sowing in a temperate northern climate. Dates and development vary by cultivar and environment. The summer view is before harvest.

Structure at specimen scale

Distichous leaves emerge at stem nodes. Their sheaths wrap the culm; a membranous ligule and paired small auricles sit at the collar. Wheat ears carry alternating spikelets along the rachis; glumes enclose the floret group, and awns arise from lemmas. Basal tillers share a crown, with seminal and crown roots and fine lateral branches.

What the detail represents

Blade ridges, longitudinal venation, glume keels, culm striation and wax variation are procedural surface cues. Stomata, epidermal cell anatomy and internal floral organs are not resolved. Root architecture is a representative branching system, truncated to a 45 cm inspection volume. Wind is a spatially coherent visual deformation with leaf flutter, not a calibrated mechanical simulation.
